Selected projects

  1. 96%Extraction Accuracy

    CAD-AI Lab, Michigan Medicine Radiology

    Fine-Tuning a 3B Clinical LLM

    Fine-tuning a small language model to extract survival predictors from bladder cancer records.

    Details

    This research compares a small language model with GPT-4 for extracting survival predictors from bladder cancer records after cystectomy.

    • The 3B-parameter model achieved 96% accuracy on the study’s clinical information-extraction task, compared with 94% for GPT-4.
    • Used LoRA fine-tuning to structure predictors from post-cystectomy bladder-cancer records.
    • We also tested whether information extracted by the model supported five-year survival prediction as well as information extracted manually.

    Blueprints for Pangaea

    Medical Supply Cataloging from Photos

    Turning photos of donated medical supplies into reviewable inventory records.

    Details

    A photo is a much easier starting point than an empty spreadsheet. The workflow turns incoming donations into records that the team can review.

  3. >50%Fewer records needing manual review

    Henry Ford Health

    Screening Papers for Systematic Reviews

    Helping researchers find relevant papers with less manual screening.

    Details

    The goal is less repetitive screening. Reviewers still get to inspect the decisions and resolve borderline papers.

    This workflow uses LLMs for first-pass paper triage, reducing manual screening burden while keeping inclusion and exclusion decisions available for human review. An expanded evaluation is currently under review.

    • Expanded the original 121-record conference study to more than 3,200 literature records.
    • Reduced the set requiring manual review by more than half through model-guided inclusion and exclusion decisions.
    • Kept structured decision logs so reviewers could inspect results and resolve borderline papers rather than treating the model as the final reviewer.

  4. 2-StageTraining Strategy

    Michigan Data Science Team

    Subtype-First Breast Cancer Classification

    Led a 10+ student team comparing direct benign/malignant training with a subtype-first approach that maps predicted tumor subtypes to the final binary label.

    Details

    Instead of training only on benign and malignant labels, the models first learned the eight tumor subtypes. Each predicted subtype was then mapped deterministically to its benign or malignant class; subtype accuracy itself was not the final endpoint.

    • Compared direct binary training with subtype-first training on the BreakHis histopathology dataset.
    • On the internal test split, subtype-first training improved binary accuracy by 23.7 percentage points for Bag of Visual Words and 11.2 points for the CNN.
    • The subtype-first ViT reported 98.9% binary accuracy and 0.989 AUC on the study split; external validation is still needed to assess generalizability.

    Project PDF PDF
